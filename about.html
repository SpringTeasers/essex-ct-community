About the town

About Essex, Connecticut

Essex is a town of 6,733 people (2020 Census) in Middlesex County, Connecticut, within the Lower Connecticut River Valley Planning Region. It is made up of three villages — Essex Village, Centerbrook, and Ivoryton — and lies on the west bank of the Connecticut River, a few miles north of Long Island Sound.

History

From Potopaug to Essex

The area now known as Essex was settled in the 17th century as part of the Saybrook Colony, and later the town of Saybrook. It was originally known as Potopaug, and later as Saybrook. It was named after Essex County, England.

Essex was incorporated as a separate town in 1852, from the Saybrook/Chester Quarter, and named Essex in 1854. Ivoryton and Centerbrook became part of Essex in 1859.

The town’s working history runs on two industries. Essex was a shipbuilding center — more than 600 vessels were built here by the Civil War era — and Ivoryton took its name from the ivory industry, with the Comstock, Cheney & Co. ivory works producing combs and piano keys.

On the Connecticut River waterfront, the Griswold Inn was established in 1776 and is one of the oldest continuously operating inns in the United States.

The Burning of the Ships, 1814

During the War of 1812, British forces raided Essex in April 1814 and burned a large number of American ships in the harbor. The event is remembered locally as the “Burning of the Ships.” The Sailing Masters of 1812 hold a commemoration every year on the second Saturday in May.

Government

Board of Selectmen and Town Meeting

Essex is governed under the Board of Selectmen / Town Meeting form of Connecticut town government — there is no mayor. The town is served by a Board of Selectmen and a Town Meeting, supported by a Town Administrator and by elected and appointed boards and commissions.

The three villages

Essex Village, Centerbrook and Ivoryton

Essex Village

The historic riverfront village and the town’s center for visitors: Main Street restaurants and inns, the museum, and the yacht basin.

  • Griswold Inn 36 Main Street · griswoldinn.com
  • River Museum 67 Main Street · ctrivermuseum.org
  • Steam Train 1 Railroad Avenue · essexsteamtrain.com

Centerbrook

A commercial and residential village along the Route 153/154 corridor, and home to much of the town’s larger commercial activity. The Cheese Shop of Centerbrook is here, at 33 Main Street.

Ivoryton

The town’s third village, east of Centerbrook along Route 153, with an arts heritage. The Ivoryton Playhouse and the Ivoryton Library are both in the village.

  • Playhouse ivorytonplayhouse.org

Library and schools

Essex Library and Regional School District #4

The Essex Library Association at 33 West Avenue (860-767-1560) serves as both library and community center, hosting author talks, children’s programs, a film festival, and summer reading. Public schooling in Essex is provided by Regional School District #4, which serves Chester, Deep River, and Essex (1 Winthrop Road, Deep River, CT 06417 · 860-526-2417).
