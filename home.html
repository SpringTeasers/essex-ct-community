Essex sits on the west bank of the Connecticut River, a few short miles from Long Island Sound. The town is home to 6,733 people (2020 Census) in Middlesex County, part of the Lower Connecticut River Valley Planning Region.

“Essex consists of three unique villages and is ideally located on the Connecticut River a few short miles from Long Island Sound. Well-known for its beautiful landscapes, majestic trees and gardens, fascinating architecture, and cultural and historical attractions, we are a boating and sailing destination as well.”

— The Town of Essex, essexct.gov/210/About

Three villages, one town

Essex Village, Centerbrook and Ivoryton

Essex Village

The historic riverfront center — Main Street, the Connecticut River Museum at 67 Main Street, the Griswold Inn at 36 Main Street (established 1776), and the Essex Steam Train & Riverboat alongside.

Centerbrook

Follows the Route 153/154 corridor and holds much of the town’s commercial and residential life, including the Cheese Shop of Centerbrook at 33 Main Street.

Ivoryton

Lies east along Route 153 and is the town’s arts village — home of the Ivoryton Playhouse and the Ivoryton Library.

What brings people here

The river, the history, and a real Main Street

  • The river. Essex is a boating and sailing destination, with its historic village built around the waterfront.
  • Living history. The Griswold Inn, established in 1776, is one of the oldest continuously operating inns in the United States. Every second Saturday in May, the Sailing Masters of 1812 commemorate the British raid of 1814 — the “Burning of the Ships.”
  • Landmarks and culture. The Connecticut River Museum, the Essex Steam Train & Riverboat, and the Ivoryton Playhouse anchor a year-round calendar.
  • Open space. Town parks and nature preserves include Bushy Hill, Canfield-Meadow Woods, Viney Hill Brook, and Essex Park, with the Essex Land Trust maintaining additional trails.
  • A real Main Street. Independent restaurants, cafés, and shops, plus a year-round calendar of library programs and community events.

Before you come to Essex

Getting here

Essex is in Middlesex County on the Connecticut River; the nearest commuter rail is Shoreline East, with stations in the Old Saybrook area. River Valley Transit provides local and regional bus service, including Dial-A-Ride and XtraMile microtransit.

Town Hall and town news

Town Hall: 29 West Avenue, Essex, CT 06426 — 860-767-4340. Town Clerk’s Office hours are Monday–Friday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m.
